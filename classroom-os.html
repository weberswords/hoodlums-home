Chapter 01

What is Classroom OS?

An operating system is the invisible layer that decides how everything else runs. Most teachers inherit theirs by accident — built from old habits, district mandates, and whatever survived the last in-service. Classroom OS was five working days to rebuild it on purpose, layer by layer: your Source Code (what you actually believe about how people learn), your Architecture (the visible structures that carry it), your Scheduler (the year as a designed arc instead of a pacing guide), and the live Applications — AI Gems, sub plans, OS Overview — that run on top. No theory. No binders. By Friday, twelve crews of teachers had built something that works whether they're in the room or not.

Chapter 02

How we worked.

The week was built the same way we were teaching them to build a classroom. We didn't start with tools. We started with who you are — not your “why,” your identity — and from day one we layered AI in by doing, not lecturing. The medium was the message.

The order of operations

Human → analog → digital

Every layer started with a person and a conversation, moved to something you could draw and hold, and only then became digital — whatever digital needed to be. The same sequence we want teachers using with students.

Day one

AI you could touch

Before any theory, teachers drew a picture, described it, and watched Adobe Firefly render it. From the first hour they were hands-on with AI — so they knew we knew what we were doing. But AI was never the base of it.

The house style

Clear rather than clever

We modeled the tools we taught — Pomodoro timers, focus apps — and showed exactly how to use them in a classroom. Psychological safety over cleverness. That's what made the week resonate.

Chapter 03

Five days, five layers.

Each morning built one layer of the system on top of the last — starting with the person, ending with something that runs without them.

Day 01 Mon · Jun 1
Empathy

Who are you as a teacher?

We didn't open with tools. We opened by asking teachers to map what they actually believe about how people learn — the real version, not the evaluation-rubric one. From a Circle Map of their teaching identity, they drafted their Source Code Document and stood up the NotebookLM that would grow with them all week.

If a stranger walked into your classroom while you were absent, what would tell them who you are?

What they built A Circle Map, the opening of a Source Code Document, and a NotebookLM ready to receive a year's worth of work.
Day 02 Tue · Jun 2
Analysis

What does your classroom actually do?

There's always a gap between the teacher you intend to be and the room you actually run. With a Bubble Map, teachers audited what was already there — procedures, layout, transitions — and started translating Source Code into visible architecture that holds without their voice carrying it. Crews pushed each other's thinking.

If you didn't remind them, would your students know what to do?

What they built A Bubble Map of the current classroom and a draft procedure set tied directly to their Source Code values.
Day 03 Wed · Jun 3
Daydreaming

What could your year actually look like?

Most teachers are handed a pacing guide and sent back to their room. Instead, teachers designed the whole year before it happened: a Flow Map of the curriculum as a sequence of intentional experiences, a working Scheduler, and their first Unit NotebookLM trained on their content and their voice.

Do your students know where they are in the year, and where they're going?

What they built A Flow Map of the full year, a working Scheduler draft, and a Unit NotebookLM in progress.
Day 04 Thu · Jun 4
Prototyping

Build the thing.

No more drafts, no more maps. Teachers built a live Google Gem — seated with their Classroom OS, trained on their content, calibrated to their students — and finalized a sub plan that runs the room without them in it. This was the day the AI went live the way it was designed to.

If you got a call tonight that you couldn't come in tomorrow, would you feel ready?

What they built A live Google Gem ready for student use and a sub plan that runs on the system, not on their presence.
Day 05 Fri · Jun 5
Testing

Does it work without you?

The whole week pointed here. In “Slopped” — equal parts AI slop and Chopped — teachers had to defend their Gem against live curveballs: “You just got a student from Ecuador who only speaks Spanish. What do you do?” A real stress test of the system against the unpredictable reality of a classroom. It held.

They already knew the answer.

What they built A complete Classroom OS, stress-tested live — and the proof it holds up when reality doesn't cooperate.

All five mornings · 9:30 AM – 12:30 PM · Legacy High School, North Las Vegas, NV

Chapter 04

The moment it was worth it.

You can describe a system all week. The teachers didn't believe it until they saw it produce — documents in their own voice, matched to their own classroom, almost effortlessly.

Case study · Zero to hero

Dawn didn't see the point. Then she did.

Dawn teaches 5th grade at Snyder ES. Wednesday afternoon, she said she didn't really see the value in customizing the Gemini Gems. By Friday she'd written: “I learned how to use AI in an effective and relevant way. I'm the human. I learned how to better communicate with AI to achieve the results I'm looking for.” In between, she built a whole classroom kit in her own brand — colors and fonts matched so everything reduces cognitive load for her students.

The peak of the week

On Thursday we ran an A/B test: the same prompt to vanilla Gemini, then the same prompt to Gemini seated with their Classroom OS. That was the moment the room could see that all the work had been worth it — documents that matched their voice and their classroom dynamics, produced almost effortlessly. That's the difference the system makes.
