Vlog & Capture Field Guide.

Five mornings of phone footage, shot to one argument: we built classrooms that run without the teacher in them — and a district should care. Every clip feeds the Pitch Film or the White Paper. If it moves neither, skip it.

The Throughline

One Question, All Week

Every before/after vlog reacts to the same question from a different angle. That's what makes five days of handheld footage edit into one story.

“Can we build a classroom that runs without the teacher in it — and make a district see why that matters?”

☀️ Morning — “Before”

  1. The Question. Say today's question out loud (see the table).
  2. The Stakes. One sentence: why a teacher or a district should care today.
  3. The Bet. “Here's what we'll build / prove by 12:30.” Name the artifact.

🌙 Evening — “After”

  1. The Callback. Re-ask the morning question.
  2. The Proof. What got built + the single best thing a teacher said.
  3. The Tease. One line at tomorrow. (Thursday: tease Friday hard.)

Host discipline: one consistent host fronts every vlog; pull a different teacher into each evening cut. By Friday the cohort has narrated its own transformation — far stronger than us narrating it for them.

The Gem Swap — The Money Shot

Each teacher swaps their Gem with someone who's never seen the content, and that person leads it through the 5 tasks. This is “does it work without you?” made visible. Shoot it like a stress test, not a celebration:

The shots

  1. Cold open: the handoff — “I built this. You've never seen it. Run my class.”
  2. The attempt: over-the-shoulder of the substitute working the Gem through 5 tasks.
  3. The verdict: turn the camera on the builder watching their class run without them. This reaction shot closes the Pitch Film.

The soundbite to land

Ask the substitute, on camera:

“Could you have run that class today with nothing but what they built?”

Their unscripted “yeah, actually” is worth more than anything we could say about ourselves.

If you get one thing clean all week, get this: steady, good audio, both faces, and shoot it landscape — the Pitch Film wants it.

The District Hook

Portrait of a Nevada Learner

A superintendent doesn't buy “cool PD.” They buy alignment to the framework they're accountable to. Our five days hit all four Portrait themes — say these words on camera; they're the district's own language.

Portrait themeHow Classroom OS proves itCapture it
Know themselvesDay 1 Source Code — naming what you believe about learningMon: Circle Map + Source Code
Connect with othersThe crew model — a chemistry teacher catching an English teacher's blind spotTue / Fri: crew + Gallery Walk
Contribute their skillsBuilding real tools (Gems, sub plans) that serve actual studentsThu: the build
Thrive through challengesA system that holds when the person isn't in the roomFri: the Gem Swap

Pitch line to record to camera:

“The Portrait of a Nevada Learner asks us to help kids know themselves, connect, contribute, and thrive. This week, the teachers did exactly that — and built classrooms that ask the same of their students. This philosophy applies to everything.”

Soundbite Bank

The Lines That Sell

Get name + grade + subject first (“I'm ___, I teach ___ at ___”). Then:

Teachers

  1. What did you walk in believing about PD, and what changed?
  2. What did you build today that you'll actually open in August?
  3. Finish: “Before this week my sub plan was ___. Now it's ___.”
  4. What could you build with this that has nothing to do with your subject?
  5. Why did you spend your summer morning here?

Admin Observers & the closer

  1. You've sat through a lot of PD. What's different about today?
  2. What would it mean for your building if every teacher had a system like this?
  3. How does what you saw connect to the Portrait of a Nevada Learner?
  4. Closer (Fri): If you were a principal, would you send your staff? Why?
