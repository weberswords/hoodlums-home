The Series

Every classroom runs on an operating system.

Most teachers inherited theirs by accident, built from old habits, mandates, and whatever survived the last in-service. It holds up until you’re absent, or exhausted, or the schedule collapses. That’s when you find out the system was you.

ClassroomOS is where you rebuild it on purpose, one layer at a time. You start with what you believe about how people learn, then build the room, the year, and the tools that carry it.

Teachers who did this in June rated their own rooms before and after. “My classroom could run without me” moved from 3.7 to 4.4, and 86% of scores went up.

The Nine Weeks

Five layers, built live.

Weeks 1 · 2 Sep 16 · Sep 23
Source Code

Who you are.

You write down what you actually believe about how people learn, so the rest of your system has something to build on.

Weeks 3 · 4 Sep 30 · Oct 7
Architecture

The visible structures.

You build the structures that keep the room running when you step away, the layout and procedures a stranger could read off your walls.

Weeks 5 · 6 Oct 14 · Oct 21
Scheduler

The year, by design.

You design your whole year as something your students can follow, instead of a pacing guide they never see.

Weeks 7 · 8 Oct 28 · Nov 4
Applications

The daily tools.

You build the tools that do the daily work, including a sub plan that runs the room the day you’re out.

Week 9 Nov 18
The Absence Test

Live curveballs.

You throw everything you built against the hard days: a new student who speaks no English arriving tomorrow, or a schedule that just fell apart.
