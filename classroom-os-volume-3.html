The Series

Every classroom runs on an operating system.

Most teachers inherited theirs by accident: old habits, district mandates, and whatever survived the last in-service. It holds up fine until you are absent, or exhausted, or the schedule collapses. Then it turns out the system was you the whole time.

ClassroomOS is where you rebuild that system on purpose, one layer at a time. You start with what you actually believe about how people learn. Then you build the room, the year, and the tools that carry it, until the design is what holds the whole thing together.

Teachers who did this in June rated their own classrooms on Monday and again on Friday. “My classroom could run without me in the room” moved from 3.7 to 4.4. Eighty-six percent of scores rose.

Why This One Runs Online

We are starting this fall, and so should you.

There is no five-day window in a school year. You know that better than we do. The teachers who built their system in June got five consecutive mornings because it was June, and the next honest chance at five straight days is Summer 2027.

Vol. III runs one hour a week on Google Meet, nine Wednesdays, this fall. Same layers. Same crews. The difference is that you will be teaching the entire time you build it.

What that changes

The summer cohorts built a system and then went home for six weeks to wonder about it. You will build a piece of yours on Wednesday and hand it to thirty students on Thursday. You see your system working while you are still teaching, and you finish with a semester of evidence.

Every session opens with what happened when you put last week’s work in front of real students.

Did anything break?
Was anything ignored?
What went well?

Then we collaborate during that hour, while your experience of it is still fresh. By Week 9 your system will have survived a quarter of real teaching.

The Nine Weeks

Same layers. Built live.

Each pair of weeks builds one layer of the system on top of the last, starting with the person and ending with something that runs without you. Here is the arc, week by week.

Weeks 1 · 2 Sep 16 · Sep 23
Source Code

Who you are.

The beliefs already running your classroom whether or not you ever named them. Your Circle Map, the opening of your Source Code Document, and the AI that will grow alongside your system all semester.

What do you actually believe about how people learn?

Weeks 3 · 4 Sep 30 · Oct 7
Architecture

The visible structures.

The structures that carry your Source Code when your voice is not carrying it. Procedures, layout, transitions. The things a stranger could read off your walls.

Could a stranger read who you are off your walls?

Weeks 5 · 6 Oct 14 · Oct 21
Scheduler

You design the year.

Where your students are, where they are going, and whether they could tell you without being told.

Do your students know where they are in the year?

Weeks 7 · 8 Oct 28 · Nov 4
Applications

The layer that does the work.

Your Gems, the thing on the wall, the resources you actually use, and a sub plan that runs the room the day you are out.

Would your room run tomorrow if you could not come in?

Week 9 Nov 11
The Absence Test

Live curveballs.

Everything you built, under pressure. A new student who speaks no English, arriving tomorrow. A schedule that just collapsed. The summer cohorts answered this from a simulation. You will answer it from a quarter of real evidence.

You already know the answer.

The Hours, and the Credit

Fifteen hours. Five CUs.

Nine of those hours are the Wednesdays. The other six are your own, spent inside your own classroom on your own system, and you log them on a spreadsheet we give you.

You will probably work more than six. Most people do. The credit caps at five CUs no matter how many hours you put in, so log what you did and stop counting.

Three copies of that log exist when this is over: yours, ours, and NV-SIDE’s. If anyone is ever audited, all three match.

What You Leave With

Nine weeks, and this is yours to keep.

  • A complete ClassroomOS: Source Code, Architecture, Scheduler, Applications.
  • An AI trained on your classroom, your content, and your voice, built up over nine weeks of real teaching.
  • A sub plan that has already been used.
  • Fifteen logged hours and five CUs.
  • A crew of four people who watched your room change in real time.
Who This Is For

The only load-bearing wall in the building.

Teachers who are already good and are tired of being the only load-bearing wall in the building. You do not need to be technical. You do not need permission. You need one hour on Wednesdays and four people willing to tell you the truth.

Where it leads

Vol. III runs this fall. Vol. IV runs in the spring. Both are online, and both lead to Vol. V, five days in person in Las Vegas in Summer 2027. Start anywhere. Teachers who run an online semester first arrive at the in-person week carrying a system that has already been through a school year, which changes what that week is for.
