01

The moment we're actually in.

[INTENT — NAME THE SOCIAL CONTEXT first: schools right now are reaching for the blunt instruments — phone bans, screen-time restriction, "no AI" policies. Those are understandable reactions to a real problem, but they're a posture, not a pedagogy. Classroom OS is the constructive third path: not banning, not unfettered adoption — "what's the right tool for the job?" The answer to restriction-or-surrender is design. Name it without scolding either camp (voice principle).]

[INTENT — THEN the PD failure mode, without blaming the teacher: one-off PD treats tools as the endpoint and the teacher as a blank slate for a vendor's feature tour. The deeper failure it leaves untouched: classrooms that run on a person's presence, energy, and voice — they don't survive the Tuesday you're out sick. That dependency is the gap this method closes. Use the one-off vs. Classroom OS contrast as a SYSTEM problem, not a teacher deficiency.]

02

The machine as a mirror.

[INTENT: The central reframe. AI reflects the teacher's expertise back — their voice, their classroom — rather than supplying or replacing it. The teacher arrives an expert; the tool has to earn its place. This is the philosophical spine the rest of the paper hangs on.]

The mirror, on day one

[INTENT: The Marcus Aurelius anecdote — teacher asks the OS "if I were a historical figure based on my OS, who would I be?" → "Because of your focus on reflection and leadership, you'd be Marcus Aurelius." Live proof of reflection, not replacement.]

Human, then analog, then digital.

[INTENT: The named "right tool for the job" scaffold and its rationale — analog protects the thinking (slows it down, lowers the barrier) before any tool speeds it up; digital then does what only digital can (translate, adapt to one learner, extend one teacher's reach, reflect work back in the teacher's voice). "Who are you?" — not "what's your why?"

[INTENT: The identity question as a deliberate design choice — identity is generative; the "why" falls out of it, not the reverse. THE ANECDOTE: after Day 1, a teacher described the question sending her back through the "historical index" of her experiences — why she became a teacher, why she's still one, what shaped who she is. The psychological power of identity, shown not asserted. Frame with dignity: it surfaced expertise she already had.]

[INTENT — how we get people thinking, with the research named but light: - OBLIQUE STRATEGIES (Eno & Schmidt, 1975) — oblique prompts to break a deadlock and provoke fresh thinking, used as a creativity technique (not a recipe). - PSYCHOLOGICAL SAFETY as a first-class constraint — opt-in, graduated disclosure; "we hate over-sharing icebreakers too"; forced vulnerability isn't safety. Tie to the SOCIAL PRESENCE MODEL (Whiteside; Garrett Dikkers): affective association + community cohesion + instructor involvement as designable conditions. - SMALL CREWS (two to four, sizes varied) as team learning (Miyake & Kirschner, 2014) — the small group as the unit of constructive, interactive engagement. - MOTIVATION designed White-Hat, not Black-Hat (Chou / Octalysis) — we engineer for durable intrinsic motivation, not compulsion loops.
